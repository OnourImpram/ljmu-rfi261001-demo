Clinical and faculty booking · §§3.3–3.4

Manage a booking

A teaching appointment needs a practitioner, an available room and, where relevant, working equipment and space under a supervisor’s allocation.

  1. Open the booking form and select the equipment marked “out of service”. Inspect which check prevents confirmation.
  2. Choose available resources and confirm. Find the booking by its reference and change it to the other available session in the example day.
  3. Cancel the booking and check that the room and kit are available again. The original reference should still identify the cancelled appointment.

Group activities · §3.4

Offer the last place

A cancellation on a full course gives a waiting participant the opportunity to take the place.

  1. In Bookings, simulate a cancellation and inspect the offer and remaining places.
  2. Claim the place in the example, then check the updated capacity and whether a second claim is available.

For implementation, LJMU would set the waiting-list policy and approved notification channels. OIV would test competing requests for the final place in the shared booking service.

Places and organisational bookings · §§3.3–3.4, 3.8

Reserve places for a group

  1. Open Course and group reservations in Bookings as the lab manager. Increase the workshop capacity so at least two places are available.
  2. Switch to the client booker and reserve two places. Compare the available count with the booking confirmation and the local message history.
  3. Try reserving more places than remain. Cancel the unbilled group reservation to release its places.

Clinical records and supervision · §§3.5–3.6

Review a student note

Choose the student role and open the assigned osteopathy case. Edit the draft, save it and inspect the available actions. Switch to the supervisor, review the amendment and countersign.

In the musculoskeletal body chart, compare front and back. Left and right refer to the patient. As the student, add an observation to the open draft and find it in the recorded list. Open the separate muscle group reference to compare a group on both views. You can switch services while writing and return to your unsaved note before choosing Save.

After countersigning, inspect the locked note and chart. Switch to the corporate booker to check that the clinical content is restricted. The supervisor’s research extract contains selected structured fields from consented example records.

The digest identifies the note reviewed. For rollout, OIV would bind each review to the clinician’s authenticated account and apply LJMU’s placement dates and retention rules.

Optional remote follow-up · §3.7

Record consent for a remote visit

  1. As the clinical supervisor, open a new osteopathy appointment and choose James Taylor under Book on behalf of. Confirm the appointment and keep its reference.
  2. Open the osteopathy episode in Clinical records. In Remote follow-up, select the appointment and prepare the patient invitation.
  3. Switch to Patient (sample) in Bookings. Open My appointment messages and accept or decline the invitation. Return as the supervisor to inspect the linked consent state and record attendance after acceptance.

The invitation, response and attendance entry are saved in the browser. With the agreed access to LJMU’s Teams tenant and messaging provider, OIV would configure the live invitation and test the patient’s journey with the clinical team.

Organisational customers · §3.8

Approve a faculty order

An athletics club hires a resource on account. Use the booker role, add a catalogue line and inspect the change in net, example VAT and gross amounts. Enter a new purchase-order reference and submit it. Switch to the manager role to approve it.

Compare the approved invoice with the order. The paying organisation and booking contact should be identifiable without including clinical notes. The example rates illustrate how a mixed basket works. LJMU Finance would set the tax treatment for each real service.

Open faculty orders →

Payments and adjustments · §3.8

Record a payment, credit and refund

  1. Open Orders as the lab manager and choose an approved invoice. Record the full amount received with a fictional external payment reference.
  2. Issue a credit note for a billed line and enter a reason. Check the new account balance before recording the corresponding refund.
  3. In Finance, select that invoice and each adjustment document. Compare the signed amounts against the original invoice and download the relevant journal.

These transactions use a fictional ledger. At implementation, OIV would test settlement and refunds with LJMU’s chosen payment provider.

Finance and governance · §§3.8–3.9

Reconcile the journal

After approval, open Finance as the manager and download the sample journal. Match its purchase-order reference to the invoice. Sum the debit and credit columns separately. Both totals should equal the approved gross amount, including the line you added.

The CSV illustrates an Oracle E-Business Suite handoff. A production acceptance test would map the accounts, import a test batch and reconcile the acknowledgement and any rejected lines.

Delivery and assurance

The proposal covers clinical services and faculty trading with shared booking and separate access to clinical records. OIV prepared this optional walkthrough for the RFI. Section 3.1 of the written response describes the proposed solution and its current production status.

LJMU would provide its service rules, approved templates, role definitions, finance codes and access to the agreed test environments. OIV would configure and test the proposed Teams, Entra ID, payment and Oracle connections with the University’s clinical, IT and finance leads.

The private RFI response sets out the phased delivery plan, dependencies, staff tiers, implementation assumptions, contract positions and options for a future tender. The service prices inside the demonstration belong to the fictional basket.
